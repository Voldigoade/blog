Autumn 2026 has exactly the kind of lineup that makes you want to say "historic" too quickly.

*Black Clover* is back. *The Apothecary Diaries* too. *Ao Ashi* returns after four years. *PSYREN* finally gets its anime. *Overgeared* arrives from the Korean webtoon. *Magic Knight Rayearth* is reborn three decades after its first series. And that's just the most visible part of the calendar: you still have to add *Dragon Ball Super: Beerus*, *Tokyo Revengers*, *Blue Box*, *Ranma 1/2*, *Steel Ball Run*, *Keroro Gunsou* and even *Suikoden*.

Taken individually, several of these titles could headline an ordinary season.

They're arriving almost all at once.

But does that make autumn 2026 a statistical anomaly in the anime industry? **No, if you simply count series. Much more likely yes, if you measure the concentration of established franchises, long-distance returns, and productions designed from the outset for an international audience.**

This distinction changes almost the entire analysis.

## First paradox: there isn't even an exceptional number of anime

The first way to test the impression of a gigantic season is to forget the names and count.

As of September 14, Oricon lists **54 productions** in its Japanese autumn 2026 calendar. That's a lot in absolute terms. Yet it's not even the year's peak: the same source counts 65 for winter 2026, 68 for spring, and 72 for summer. Autumn 2025 had 61. The scope of such catalogs is never perfectly neutral—short formats, streaming distribution, sequel cours, and foreign productions can influence the total—but using a single source at least allows a relatively coherent comparison.

In other words, **autumn 2026 is not a volume anomaly**.

With its current count, it even contains 18 fewer titles than the summer preceding it.

That matters, because it immediately eliminates an intuitive explanation: the impression of excess doesn't come simply from an exceptional traffic jam of new series.

It comes from their **relative weight**.

## The real phenomenon is a concentration of extremely recognizable intellectual properties

You only have to look at what the number 54 masks.

On October 2, *The Apothecary Diaries Season 3*, *Overgeared*, and the new arc of *Tokyo Revengers* arrive simultaneously. October 3 follows with *Black Clover*. October 4 brings *Ao Ashi Season 2* and *Blue Box Season 2*. October 7, the new *Magic Knight Rayearth*. *PSYREN* starts October 5. *Dragon Ball Super: Beerus* is scheduled for October 11.

Alongside that, Netflix begins weekly streaming on September 25 of the eleven episodes covering the second and third stages of *Steel Ball Run*, the new adaptation of *JoJo's Bizarre Adventure*. The platform is also launching *Blue Box Season 2* in October, while *Ranma 1/2* returns for a third season.

And Oricon adds *Keroro Gunsou☆*, *Suikoden*, *Ace of the Diamond act II -Second Season-*, several sequels, and a quantity of less-publicized productions.

So it's not exactly "many more anime."

It's rather **an abnormally large share of media space occupied by names that already have a history, a fandom, or an international market**.

That's the phenomenon that visually crushes the rest of the calendar.

## Black Clover perfectly illustrates the compression effect

The *Black Clover* case is particularly revealing.

Episode 170, then presented as the final episode of the TV series, aired on **March 30, 2021**. The new season will begin on **October 3, 2026**. More than five and a half years therefore separate the two TV broadcasts, even though the film *Sword of the Wizard King* occupied part of the interval. Director Ayataka Tanemura, already involved in the first series and the film, returns to direct.

This isn't simply a season 2 arriving a year after the previous one.

It's the reintroduction of a major shōnen franchise that had left the weekly TV rendezvous since 2021.

And it's not alone in playing that card.

*Ao Ashi* returns four years after its first 2022 adaptation. Its author explicitly acknowledges on the official site that four years have passed and that some readers had given up on the idea of a sequel. The return comes with a notable change: Production I.G animated the first season; **TMS Entertainment is producing the second**, slated for 24 episodes.

This difference matters. A franchise can return, but the word "return" guarantees neither aesthetic continuity nor production continuity.

## PSYREN pushes the phenomenon much further

*PSYREN* constitutes an even more singular case.

Toshiaki Iwashiro's manga ran in *Weekly Shonen Jump* between 2007 and 2010. It had never gotten a TV series. More than fifteen years after its conclusion, SATELIGHT finally adapts it, with Katsumi Ono directing. The project has furthermore announced its intention to adapt the story through to its conclusion.

So this isn't a contemporary license propelled into anime after two years of promising sales.

It's practically the opposite: **a manga finished for sixteen years that benefits from a collective memory persistent enough to belatedly justify a complete adaptation**.

It's hard to imagine a better demonstration of autumn 2026's particular character.

## And Rayearth returns after thirty-one years

The former *Magic Knight Rayearth* TV adaptation began on October 17, 1994 and ended in November 1995 after 49 episodes. The new series launches on **October 7, 2026** with E&H production handling animation, TMS steering the project, and Yuki Kajiura, Taku Iwasaki... no: here the official sheet credits **Yuki Kajiura, Takumi Ozawa and Shiho Terada** for music.

Thirty-one years therefore separate the two TV launches.

*Keroro Gunsou☆* follows a similar logic: Bandai Namco Pictures will launch a new TV series for the franchise on October 3, after a long period without a new regular series.

At this stage, a structure emerges: **autumn 2026 doesn't just stack sequels. It compresses several generations of the industry into the same quarter.**

Properties from the 1990s rub shoulders with 2000s shōnen, 2010s hits, and franchises born in the manga, light novel, or webtoon ecosystem of the 2020s.

That's far less common than a simple season with 60 or 70 new titles.

## Even Dragon Ball participates in this strange reactivation logic

It would be misleading, however, to present *Dragon Ball Super: Beerus* as a brand-new sequel.

Toei and the official *Dragon Ball* site describe it as an **"Enhanced Edition"** of the *Dragon Ball Super* anime. The project revisits existing material with numerous new cuts, revised scenes, new rendering, re-recorded dubbing, reworked music and effects, and a restructuring of the story. Broadcast begins October 11.

That's precisely what makes it interesting in this analysis.

The season simultaneously welcomes traditional sequels, a long-impossible adaptation, remakes, catalog reactivations, and even a modern reconstruction of a recent anime.

Autumn 2026 looks less like a wave of novelties than **a massive reintroduction of already-proven intellectual properties**.

## But the other half of the season is precisely trying to create the next franchises

The calendar isn't entirely turned toward the past.

*Overgeared* is the most obvious example. The adaptation of the Korean webtoon is directed by Ayako Kono at J.C.STAFF, with Kenta Ihara on series composition. Crunchyroll has secured near-worldwide distribution, excluding Japan and Korea.

*Firefly Wedding* arrives at david production. *Suikoden* transposes a particularly old video game franchise at KONAMI animation. Several other adaptations occupy studios like WHITE FOX, [P.A.Works](http://P.A.Works) or SILVER LINK.

This is where a second misleading impression needs tempering.

A list of known studios doesn't automatically mean all these series will benefit from exceptional production.

The autumn aligns Pierrot, OLM, TMS, J.C.STAFF, SATELIGHT, david production, MAPPA and E&H production, but schedules, internal teams, directors, animation producers, and production conditions count for more than the logo placed after the credits.

**The season possesses an impressive industrial pedigree; it does not yet possess a collective guarantee of quality.**

## The Apothecary Diaries plays in another category

*The Apothecary Diaries* is neither a resurrection nor a forgotten gamble.

It's a franchise currently in full expansion.

The official portal announces more than **57 million cumulative copies** for the series and explicitly presents its success as both national and international. Season 3 will begin October 2 at OLM. Especially, a film will release in Japan as early as **December 11, 2026**, with an original story proposed by author Natsu Hyūga.

TV season and film of the same license will therefore overlap during the same quarter.

This gives autumn 2026 another characteristic: some of its headliners aren't just returning to the screen; they're occupying multiple formats at once.

## Internationalization enormously amplifies the "super-season" impression

Twenty years ago, two major anime programmed simultaneously in Japan didn't necessarily provoke the same global phenomenon.

In 2026, almost all major releases are immediately placed in international circuits.

Crunchyroll confirmed today *Black Clover*, *The Apothecary Diaries*, *Ao Ashi*, *Overgeared*, *PSYREN* and *Magic Knight Rayearth* in its autumn catalog. For several of them, rights cover the Americas, Europe, Africa, Oceania, the Middle East, and large parts of Asia. The platform also plans English dubs for a significant portion of this selection.

Netflix will stream *Steel Ball Run* every Friday from September 25 and presents the season as a global release.

*Tokyo Revengers: Three Deities Arc* will, for its part, be offered worldwide on SVOD via Disney+, excluding mainland China, in parallel with its Japanese broadcast.

The result is important: competition between series is no longer just competition for a Japanese timeslot or a magazine's readership.

**Several of the biggest titles will find themselves before a large portion of the international audience during the same weeks.**

This distribution architecture transforms a strong Japanese season into an almost instantaneous global event.

## Yet autumn 2022 prevents calling it an unprecedented season

To know if 2026 is truly extraordinary, you need a serious opponent.

Autumn 2022 is hard to bypass.

Within weeks of each other were programmed *Chainsaw Man*, the second part of *SPY×FAMILY*, *My Hero Academia Season 6*, *Mob Psycho 100 III*, *Bleach*'s return with *Thousand-Year Blood War*, *Blue Lock*, *Mobile Suit Gundam: The Witch from Mercury*, *Golden Kamuy Season 4*, *To Your Eternity Season 2*, *JoJo: Stone Ocean* and several other productions. *Cyberpunk: Edgerunners* had also just launched in September.

And this comparison reveals an essential element.

Before its broadcast, *Bocchi the Rock!* wasn't necessarily perceived as one of that season's historical pillars. *Blue Lock* hadn't yet demonstrated the scale of its animated impact. A season can become exceptional **thanks to series we don't yet know will explode**.

That's precisely the problem when trying to judge autumn 2026 on September 15.

We know its prior commercial power.

We don't yet know its surprises.

## Autumn 2024 provides a second counter-example

2024 had also produced an impressive concentration: *Dragon Ball DAIMA*, *Re:ZERO Season 3*, *DAN DA DAN*, *Blue Lock Season 2*, *Bleach: Thousand-Year Blood War Part 3*, *Blue Box*, the new *Ranma 1/2* adaptation, *Orb: On the Movements of the Earth* and several other series shared the quarter.

You already find the formula that gives 2026 its extraordinary appearance today:

an old global franchise, several highly anticipated sequels, a nostalgic remake, and a few new adaptations capable of becoming the next big licenses.

Autumn 2026 doesn't create this configuration.

It pushes it particularly far.

## And 2023 reminds why pre-broadcast comparison remains dangerous

Autumn 2023 notably brought together the debuts of *Frieren* and *The Apothecary Diaries*, while *SPY×FAMILY*, *Dr. Stone* and *Jujutsu Kaisen* also occupied the season or continued their runs.

Retrospectively, associating *Frieren* and *The Apothecary Diaries* in the same quarter seems almost absurd given how important both franchises have become.

But precisely: part of that importance wasn't measurable before their broadcast.

This asymmetry biases all comparisons with 2026. Past seasons are evaluated with several years of hindsight; autumn 2026 is evaluated with trailers, studio names, and expectations.

Comparing their final reputation would be methodologically unsound.

## So what's actually abnormal in 2026?

Not the number of series.

Not even the existence of several major licenses: 2022 and 2024 suffice to show that's already happened.

The particularity lies rather in the **combination of three layers** that rarely coexist with such density.

The first is immediate power: *The Apothecary Diaries*, *Black Clover*, *Dragon Ball*, *JoJo*, *Tokyo Revengers*, *Blue Box*.

The second is resurrection: *Magic Knight Rayearth*, *Keroro*, *PSYREN*, *Ao Ashi* after four years, *Black Clover* after more than five years.

The third is renewal: *Overgeared*, *Firefly Wedding*, *Suikoden* and the dozens of other adaptations attempting to become the dominant licenses of subsequent seasons.

It's this mixture that makes the calendar appear disproportionate.

It doesn't contain more slots.

**It contains more slots whose names already mean something before the first episode even airs.**

## The decisive test will only begin in October

A major license can produce a mediocre adaptation. A resurrected franchise can find its audience belongs to the past. A cult manga can turn out much harder to adapt than to demand for fifteen years. A studio change can work admirably or profoundly alter a series' visual identity.

Conversely, a production currently relegated to the background can become the season's true event.

That's why labeling autumn 2026 "best season in years" right now would mostly be turning anticipation into conclusion.

The data authorizes something more precise.

**Autumn 2026 is not a quantitative anomaly. It is, however, unusually dense in international franchises and especially in very long-distance returns.**

It looks less like a production record than a convergence point: three decades of Japanese anime seem to have decided to meet again in the same quarter.

To become a truly historic season, it still lacks the only measure impossible to obtain before October: what these anime will actually be worth once animated.
